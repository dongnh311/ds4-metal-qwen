### Why the longctx suite was rerun

The arm's first longctx pass hit every needle but had 22,216 swap-outs and prefilled the 480K
prompt at 283 t/s. A 96.5 GB model download from another session
(`gguf/glm53/download.log`: `DogContext/GLM-5.3-Flash-Uncensored-Q2-ds4`, 16:33:25-17:09:59, then a
full sha256 read) overlapped the 480K prompt (16:22-16:50):
- the arm's prefill took 85-99 s per 32K tokens before 16:33 and 122-146 s after it;
- probe D, the same config alone on a fresh server with no other load, took 61-76 s throughout.

| run (480K needle, arm config) | continued checkpoints | prefill | swap-outs | peak wired |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| arm, first pass, download overlapping | SP4 policy | 283.4 t/s | 22,216 | 45.65 GiB |
| probe D, fresh server, alone | SP4 policy (26 stores) | 459.5 t/s | 0 | 45.65 GiB |
| probe E, fresh server, alone | off | 473.1 t/s | 0 | 45.72 GiB |
| rerun, after 120K/240K/docqa | SP4 policy | 410.1 t/s | 0 | 45.59 GiB |

- D against E: the SP4 checkpoint policy costs 3% of prefill at 480K and no swap.
- E against the morning's probe B (485 t/s, no steering): the projection costs about 2.5%.
- The rerun against D: two earlier long prompts on the same server cost about 11% on the 480K
  prefill, with no swap.
- Not explained: before 16:33 the arm was already about 30% slower than probe D, while the rerun's
  same sequence cost only 11%. The rerun logged the top CPU processes every minute; the arm's first
  pass had no such log, so another load before 16:33 cannot be ruled in or out.

The first pass stays in the run directory (`rows.before-rerun-*.jsonl`,
`server.before-rerun-longctx.log`, `summary.before-rerun-longctx.json`); `vmstat-rerun-lc.log` in
`ds4-metal-data/sp4` shows the swap-out counter flat over the whole rerun.

## Verdict

**Pass as a separate 512K entry.** The long-context exit checks all hold. The only regression is
the short-prompt `reason` suite under YaRN 2, which the spec answers by keeping the 262K default
and adding 512K (`-c 524288`) as its own registry entry. Adding that entry is a PROD change and waits
for the user's approval and the `prod/<feature>-YYYYMMDD` deploy path.
